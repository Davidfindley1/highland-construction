# Roofing research notes (November 2026)

Internal notes for the Highland Construction site. Figures below are taken from the linked sources and were current when checked on 24 November 2026. They are national or manufacturer figures, not Highland quotes. Costs vary by roof size, slope, complexity, tear-off, deck repairs, and region — always invite a free estimate rather than invent a local price.

## IKO Dynasty and IKO Nordic — they are asphalt, not stone-coated metal

This was verified on IKO's own product pages.

**IKO Dynasty** is a high-performance *laminated asphalt shingle* (fiberglass mat, thick traditional asphalt coating, ceramic granules). It is part of IKO's Performance collection.

- Official page: https://www.iko.com/na/product/dynasty/
- Class 3 impact resistance (FM 4473 / UL 2218)
- Limited wind warranty: 130 mph (210 km/h)
- Limited Lifetime warranty; Iron Clad Protection: 15 years; blue-green algae resistance: 10 years
- Standards include ASTM D3462, D3018, D3161 Class F, D7158 Class H, and ASTM E108 / UL 790 Class A fire resistance when installed over an approved underlayment
- ArmourZone: wider reinforced nailing strip; FastLock sealant (from IKO brochures)
- IKO states the impact rating is for possible insurance-premium discounts and is *not* a guarantee of hail performance. Hail damage is **not** covered under the IKO Limited Warranty.

**IKO Nordic** is also a *laminated asphalt shingle*, not metal. IKO describes it as its highest-performing asphalt line. It uses **polymer-modified asphalt** on a fiberglass mat (more flexible in the cold than standard asphalt).

- Official page: https://www.iko.com/na/product/nordic/
- Class 4 impact resistance (FM 4473 / UL 2218) — highest class for this test
- Limited wind warranty: 130 mph; Limited Lifetime; Iron Clad 15 years; algae 10 years
- Same caveat: impact rating is not a hail-performance guarantee; hail damage is not covered under the IKO Limited Warranty
- **Available only in select markets** (stated on the product page)
- IKO blog positions Nordic for hail-prone, cold regions (Great Plains, Upper Midwest, Canadian Prairies) and Dynasty more toward high-wind / coastal use: https://www.iko.com/na/blog/storm-proofing-your-roof/
- Brochure (Nordic): https://www.iko.com/na/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/IKO_NRDC_SHGL_BROC_HMWR_CA.pdf
- IKO U.S. limited warranty (installations on or after 1 February 2025): https://www.iko.com/na/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/IKO-Limited-Warranty-U.S.pdf

**Corporate relationship:** DECRA stone-coated steel is sold as “DECRA by IKO Metals.” IKO therefore makes both premium laminated asphalt shingles (Dynasty, Nordic) and, through IKO Metals, stone-coated steel (DECRA). They are related brands, not the same product. Never label Dynasty or Nordic as stone-coated metal.

## How stone-coated steel roofing is made

From DECRA's contractor brochure and ICC-ES ESR-1754:

1. Structural-quality sheet steel complying with **ASTM A792**, typically **AZ50** aluminum-zinc alloy coating (the coating family often sold under trade names such as Galvalume®). ESR-1754: coated steel thickness **0.017 in (0.43 mm)**.
2. Corrosion-inhibiting / acrylic primer on the steel.
3. An **acrylic resin base coat** on the exposed face.
4. **Colored stone granules** (ceramic-coated) embedded in the base coat.
5. A **clear acrylic glaze / overglaze**.

Sources:

- ICC-ES ESR-1754 (reissued November 2025, renewal November 2026): https://decra.com/hubfs/Code%20Compliance/ESR-1754-Nov-2025-Nov-2026.pdf
  - Installed weight: **1.3 psf (6.3 kg/m²)**
  - Recognized as metal roof shingles under IBC 1507.5 and IRC R905.4
  - Fire classification of the *installed assembly* can be **Class A, B, or C** (ASTM E108 or UL 790), depending on underlayment and/or barrier board — do not say every DECRA roof is automatically Class A
  - Minimum slopes in the report: **Shingle XD 4:12**; Villa Tile, Shake XD, and Shingle Plus **3:12** (and a limited decorative-overlay path at lower slopes that we should not treat as a standard reroof)
  - Minimum deck typically 15/32-inch plywood or other code-compliant solid sheathing
- DECRA contractor brochure (composition and install methods): https://decra.com/hubfs/Website%20Files/Brochures/Decra_Contractor_Brochure_2023.pdf
- DECRA specification sheet (composition list: steel with aluminum-zinc hot-dip coating, acrylic priming, acrylic basecoat, ceramic-coated stone granules, acrylic over-glaze): https://www.decra.com/hubfs/DECRA%20Roofing%20Spec%20Sheet_17x11.pdf
- Independent explainer of the layer stack (aluminum-zinc / Galvalume or Zincalume, acrylic primer, basecoat, granules, overglaze): PDH-Pro “Stone Coated Steel Roofs,” https://www.pdh-pro.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/SU-02-403.pdf
- Metal Construction News overview (lightweight metal, shingle/shake/tile/slate looks; batten or direct-to-deck): https://www.metalconstructionnews.com/articles/the-stone-coated-steel-roofing-system/

## DECRA profiles and installation method

From the 2023 contractor brochure and ESR-1754:

| Profile | Look | Typical installation (per DECRA brochure) | Min. slope (ESR-1754) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DECRA Shingle XD | Architectural / thick-cut shingle | Direct-to-deck | 4:12 |
| DECRA Shake XD | Wood-shake look | Direct-to-deck | 3:12 |
| DECRA Villa Tile | Barrel / Old-World tile look | Direct-to-deck | 3:12 |
| DECRA Tile | Tile look (classic) | Battens | (follow current DECRA Tile guide) |
| DECRA Shake | Shake look (classic) | Battens | (follow current DECRA Shake guide) |
| DECRA Shingle Plus | Shingle-look panel listed in ESR-1754 | Listed in the evaluation report | 3:12 |

Direct-to-deck profiles (Villa Tile, Shingle XD, Shake XD) use DECRA’s “Cut and Tuck” accessory approach and do not require battens. Classic Tile and Shake are installed on battens.

Sources:

- https://decra.com/hubfs/Website%20Files/Brochures/Decra_Contractor_Brochure_2023.pdf
- Product pages: https://www.decra.com/metal-shingle-roofing and https://www.decra.com/metal-shake-roofing
- Profile explainer: https://www.decra.com/blog/which-decra-roofing-product-is-right-for-you
- XD install guide: https://decra.com/hubfs/DECRA%20Install%20Guide%20-%20XD%20081823.pdf
- Tile and Shake install guide (freeze/thaw: use ice and water shield in cold climates; foot traffic in pans, not high points; walk over battens when battens are used): https://decra.com/hubfs/DECRA%20Install%20Guide%20-%20Tile%20and%20Shake%20102422.pdf
- Walking instructions (soft-soled shoes; avoid side laps; designated stepping zones by profile): https://www.decra.com/hubfs/1-DECRA%20Technical%20Bulletin%20-%20DECRA%20Roof%20System%20Walking%20Instructions.pdf

Battens vs direct-to-deck (context, not a reason to ignore the product’s listed method):

- Use the method specified for that profile. Wind, fire, and warranty coverage apply to the tested assembly, not to a mix-and-match install.
- Battens create an air space; DECRA and trade articles note ventilation / walking-on-battens benefits. Batten installs typically cost more (HomeGuide). Direct-to-deck is what DECRA specifies for the XD and Villa Tile lines and is often chosen where a slimmer assembly or high-wind fastening schedule is required (HomeGuide also notes direct-to-deck as common in high-wind work).

## Warranties, wind, hail, fire (DECRA)

- **Lifetime Limited Warranty** for single-family detached residential use in the U.S. and Canada; **50-year limited** for multi-family and non-residential (DECRA / installer literature; confirm against the current warranty PDF).
- Wind: manufacturing-defect coverage related to panel blow-off in winds **up to 120 mph**.
- Hail: coverage related to **penetration** by hailstones **2.5 inches or smaller**. Normal weathering and minor granule loss are excluded. After year 20, labor is not included; later years are prorated on materials. Transfer: the warranty may be transferred to a subsequent owner **within 20 years** of original installation. Registration is not required for coverage.
- Warranty PDF (October 2025): https://www.decra.com/hubfs/Website%20Files/Warranty/DECRA%20Warranty%20Final.pdf
- Registration / summary: https://www.decra.com/register-warranty

**UL 2218 Class 4:** DECRA states that all panel styles (Shingle XD, Shake XD, Villa Tile, Shake, Tile, and Shingle Plus) meet UL 2218 Class 4. The Class 4 test drops a 2-inch steel ball twice in the same spot without tearing/fracturing/cracking that would open the panel. An indent or local granule loss can be aesthetic rather than a functional failure. DECRA also cites older NBS Series 23 ice-sphere testing.

- https://www.decra.com/pro/hail-understanding-impact-resistance

Do not call any roof “hail-proof.” Distinguish DECRA’s *penetration* warranty from IKO asphalt’s impact *rating* (which does not add hail coverage to the IKO shingle warranty).

**Fire:** assembly-dependent Class A, B, or C per ESR-1754. The steel itself is non-combustible, but the installed roof classification depends on underlayment / barrier board.

## Weight, walkability, noise

- Weight: **1.3 psf** installed (ESR-1754), about 130 lb per roofing square. Far lighter than clay or concrete tile. Lighter than a typical asphalt shingle roof, though exact asphalt weights vary by product.
- Walkability: walkable with care, not a sidewalk. DECRA’s technical bulletin maps stepping zones by profile; avoid side laps; soft-soled shoes. The buying guide addresses the myth that you cannot walk on metal, while still requiring ordinary roof-safety practice.
- Noise: DECRA’s buying guide says the stone coating and texture disrupt sound and that soundproofing underlayment is not required for noise. HomeGuide says a correct install (deck, underlayment, insulation, and the granule coat; battens add an air space) is typically as quiet as, or quieter than, asphalt, but a home with little or no attic may still benefit from a sound-dampening underlayment. Present both the manufacturer position and the “it depends on the assembly” caution. Do not promise a silent roof.
  - Buying guide: https://decra.com/hubfs/decra-metal-roofing-web-buying-guide.pdf

## Snow, ice dams, and Midwest climate

Ice dams form when heat escaping into the attic melts snow on the upper roof; the water refreezes at the cold eaves and backs up under the roof covering. The durable fix is air sealing, insulation, and a cold roof / proper ventilation — not the surface material alone.

- UMass Building and Construction Technology, Paul Fisette, “Preventing Ice Dams”: https://www.umass.edu/bct/publications/articles/preventing-ice-dams/
- DOE Building America / PNNL: https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/construct-roofs-and-attics-ice-dam-prevention
- DOE Building Science Education (eaves in cold climates): https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/eaves-sealed-cold-climates

DECRA’s Tile and Shake installation guide tells installers to use ice-and-water shield as recommended for cold climates. A textured stone-coated surface can hold snow longer than a slick standing-seam panel; neither material “prevents” ice dams if the attic is leaky and under-insulated. Ice-and-water membrane at eaves (and valleys) remains good practice in Northern Indiana and Southern Michigan.

Hail occurs in the Midwest; UL 2218 Class 4 and DECRA’s 2.5-inch penetration term are relevant talking points, with the usual “not hail-proof” caveat.

## What we could not verify (or should not claim)

- Highland is **not** described as a certified, authorized, or factory-trained DECRA or IKO installer. Do not invent certifications, warranties-from-Highland, years in roofing, or job counts.
- Local installed prices for Northern Indiana / Southern Michigan were not published in a source we could cite; use national ranges only.
- Exact current list pricing from DECRA or IKO was not published as a consumer unit price on the pages reviewed.
- Precise installed weight for IKO Dynasty / Nordic (lb per square) was not taken from a technical data sheet in this pass.
- “Class A fire” is assembly-dependent for DECRA; do not over-claim.
- HomeGuide mentions 24- or 28-gauge options in places; DECRA’s current ESR-1754 figure is 0.017 in (26-gauge range). Prefer the evaluation report over secondary gauge claims for DECRA.
- Whether IKO Nordic is actually stocked for a given Indiana or Michigan town must be checked at the time of a bid (“select markets”).
- Long-term total-cost-of-ownership for a specific house cannot be computed without that roof’s size, layers, and remaining life. Explain the logic with ranges; do not invent a payback year.
- Insurance premium discounts for Class 3 / Class 4 roofs are possible in some markets and are not guaranteed.
